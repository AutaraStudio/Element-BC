Project Overview

This brief outlines the design direction for the new Element Building Consultancy website. The goal is to create a refined, text-driven digital presence that communicates quiet confidence, precision, and control — reflecting the way Element operates with their clients.

The client's number one directive is that less is more. While they love their brand guidelines, they feel the full palette and visual system is too busy for the website. The site should strip things back, prioritise clarity, and let the strength of Element's message and work speak for itself.

⚡ Design Philosophy

Quiet confidence. The site should feel like a perfectly composed space — not loud, not competing for attention, but unmistakably premium. Think of walking into a well-designed building: everything is considered, nothing is accidental.

Design Goals & Direction

The website needs to do one thing exceptionally well: communicate that Element takes control of the process with a personalised approach, and that working with them means certainty of outcomes. The design should be primarily text-driven, conveying the value of their services through confident, precise language rather than visual noise.

Primary Design Objectives

What the design must achieve

Less is More — Restrained, refined, and intentional. Every element earns its place.
Text-Driven — Value is conveyed through language. Typography leads the experience.
Quiet Confidence — Not loud or competing for attention. Unmistakably premium.
Personalised Approach — Users must understand Element adapts to each project and client.
Simplify Complexity — The site itself should embody what Element does: bring clarity to complexity.
Subtle Brand Presence — Hints of brand colour, not full sections. Restraint is key.

🎯 Target: "Confident you can deliver"

A potential client should spend 30 seconds on the site and feel confident that Element can deliver. The design should communicate control, precision, and trustworthiness without saying so explicitly.

✨ Target: "Excited to work with you"

The second respondent wants the site to make people excited to work with Element. The design should feel approachable and human despite the premium finish — not cold or corporate.

04

Visual Direction & Style

Minimal Refined Quiet Confidence Text-Driven Architectural Premium Not Busy Not Loud
📐 Layout

Clean, generous whitespace. Let content breathe. Interesting grid layouts for the project showcase, but the overall feel should be open and uncluttered. Think architectural — considered proportions and intentional negative space.

🔤 Typography

Typography should do the heavy lifting. The brand font is Adelphi PE Variable (use Arial as digital fallback). Headlines should be confident and measured — not aggressive. Body copy should be easy to read with generous line-height.

🎨 Colour Restraint

Predominantly neutral with subtle brand colour accents. The only full-colour section should be the page load animation. Throughout the rest of the site, colour is used sparingly — a tinted line, an accent on hover, a soft wash behind a section.

📸 Photography

Premium, crisp architectural and interior photography. Depth-of-field for foreground detail. Natural daylight is preferred. Detail close-ups should be woven throughout to reinforce the "attention to detail" message.

Client directive: They currently feel the brand guidelines are too busy. The website should be a more restrained expression of the brand — focused on values, key messaging, and their personalised approach. Think of the website as the "quiet" side of the brand.

Website Colour Usage Direction
Primary Background

White or very light warm neutral (off-white, Stone-tinted). The site should feel light and airy.

Text

Dark Blue (#111b31) or Charcoal (#272726) for headings and body. Not pure black — softer and more refined.

Accent — Subtle Only

Egg Blue or Red used sparingly: hover states, thin rules, small UI details, the page loader. Never large blocks of colour.

Footer / Dark Mode

Dark Blue or Charcoal for the GSAP colour-change footer section. This is the one area where a dark palette takes over.

Typography

Brand Font: Adelphi PE Variable

Headlines: Adelphi PE Variable in heavier weights. Body copy: Adelphi PE Variable in Thin, Light, or Regular. Digital fallback: Arial. For feature headlines, the brand guidelines specify inserting 5 spaces between words for a distinctive style — consider whether this works on web or whether generous letter-spacing achieves a similar effect.

06

Page Load & Animations

The site should have a considered, minimal approach to animation — keeping in line with the "less is more" ethos. Every animation should feel deliberate, never gratuitous. The two key interaction moments are the page load and the footer colour change.

Page Load Animation

The one moment where full brand colour is used

Full-colour background — Egg Blue, Dark Blue, or a combination from the brand palette
Logo animates in — Smooth, elegant entrance. Not fast — measured and confident.
Tagline reveals — "Every detail managed." fades or slides in beneath the logo.
Reveal transition — The loader lifts, wipes, or dissolves to reveal the home page content behind.
Reference: The client specifically cited basha-franklin.com's loading animation as inspiration — the simplicity of the landing page combined with a smooth, premium animation on entry.

GSAP Footer Colour Change

Theme shift on scroll as footer comes into view

Scroll-triggered — As the footer enters the viewport, the body background transitions smoothly
Dark palette — Background shifts to Dark Blue (#111b31) or Charcoal (#272726)
Text colour inverts — All text in the footer area shifts to light/white tones
Seamless transition — The change should feel smooth and considered, not abrupt
Additional Animation Notes

📝 General Approach

Beyond the page load and footer, keep animations minimal: gentle fade-ins on scroll, subtle hover states, and smooth page transitions. Nothing should feel like it's trying to impress — it should feel like the site simply works beautifully. Annotate all intended animations in the Figma file.

Design Guidelines

Follow these guidelines to ensure the mockups align with the project vision and are ready for client presentation.

Figma Setup

  • Design at 1440px desktop width
  • Use the dummy content provided — avoid lorem ipsum
  • Name all layers clearly for future handoff
  • Use placeholder photography that reflects the image direction
  • Include a notes layer or Figma comments for animation annotations

Visual Rules

  • Less is more — if an element doesn't earn its place, remove it
  • Predominantly light/neutral backgrounds with subtle colour accents
  • Typography-led design — let text do the work
  • Generous whitespace throughout
  • Full brand colour used only on the page load; hints elsewhere
  • Footer dark section with GSAP colour-change transition

Things to Avoid

  • Too many colours — don't use the full brand palette at once
  • Cluttered layouts — every section should feel spacious
  • Loud or competing visual elements
  • Generic consultancy aesthetics — this should feel distinctive
  • Excessive imagery — text should lead, photography supports

Things to Embrace

  • Quiet confidence and restraint
  • Interesting, asymmetric grid layouts for the project showcase
  • Strong typographic hierarchy
  • Architectural precision in spacing and alignment
  • Moments of subtle delight — a soft hover, a considered transition
